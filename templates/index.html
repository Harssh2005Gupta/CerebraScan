TAXONOMY

Classification Scope & Lesion Classes

The NeuroMap model is trained to categorize brain MRI scans into four mutual classes:

Glioma

Primary intra-axial brain tumors originating from glial progenitor cells (astrocytomas, oligodendrogliomas, glioblastomas). Often characterized by ill-defined, infiltrative margins and surrounding vasogenic edema.

Meningioma

Predominantly benign, extra-axial neoplasms originating from arachnoid cap cells of the meninges. Typically well-circumscribed, displaying homogeneous contrast enhancement and a characteristic dural tail sign.

Pituitary Tumor

Neuroendocrine neoplasms arising in the sella turcica from the anterior pituitary gland (adenohypophysis). Frequently present with endocrine dysregulation or optic chiasm compression causing bitemporal hemianopsia.

No Tumor (Normal)

Absence of focal mass effect, abnormal contrast enhancement, or gross structural pathology. Represents healthy brain parenchyma, baseline ventricular symmetry, and normal anatomical margins.

LIMITATIONS

Technical Limitations & Clinical Safety

Understanding model constraints is crucial to preventing automation bias and misinterpretation:

🎯

Receptive Field Blooming

Grad-CAM operates on late convolutional feature maps (7×7 spatial resolution). Bilinear upsampling to 224×224 produces coarse heat blobs that highlight influential regions rather than precise anatomical tumor boundaries or surgical resection margins.

🧊

2D Single-Slice Limitation

Clinical neuroradiology interprets volumetric multi-parametric sequences (T1-weighted, T1-contrast, T2-weighted, and FLAIR). NeuroMap analyzes a single 2D slice without volumetric depth, multi-sequence contrast, or patient clinical history.

⚠️

Selection Bias & Demographics

Trained on public benchmark datasets with potential slice redundancy and demographic clustering. The model has not been validated on diverse hospital populations, rare tumor subtypes, or low-grade gliomas.

🔒

Privacy & Data Handling

Uploaded scans are processed in-memory for inference. Temporary visual overlays are automatically purged from storage via automated lifecycle management. No patient-identifiable data (PHI) is collected or retained.
